      29              :                  diameter_distribution=nothing, length_distribution=nothing) -> ScalingTree
      30              : 
      31              : A symmetric tree of `depth` bifurcations whose vessel diameters and lengths follow measured
      32              : scaling laws: each generation has a mean diameter and a mean length set by two exponents, and
      33              : every vessel is drawn about those means with its own generation's spread.
      34              : 
      35              : **Why these statistics.** A vessel's Poiseuille resistance goes as `L/D⁴`, so the
      36              : per-generation statistics of `D` and `L` set where along the tree the pressure drops and how
      37              : unevenly flow divides among the leaves.
      38              : 
      39              : - **Diameter.** A fork conserves `D^γ`, `D_parent^γ = Σ D_daughter^γ`, so generation `g` (`0`
      40              :   the inlet) has mean `D̄_g = leaf_diameter · 2^((depth − g)/γ)`. Murray's `γ = 3` minimizes
      41              :   pumping plus blood-volume cost (Murray 1926), and arterioles below about 100 µm obey it:
      42              :   `Q ∝ D^3.01` (99.9 % CI 2.86–3.14) over 6–108 µm in rat cremaster (Mayrovitz & Roy 1983),
      43              :   and `x ≈ 3` in peripheral resistance arteries across studies (Nakamura & Awa 2014). The
      44              :   smaller values reported for large trees, `Q ∝ D^(7/3)` (Huo & Kassab 2012) or about `D²` in
      45              :   pig coronaries (Choy & Kassab 2008), regress each subtree's stem flow on its stem diameter
      46              :   across a whole tree, which is not a per-fork exponent. A lower `γ` widens each parent
      47              :   against its daughters and moves resistance toward the leaves.
      48              : - **Length.** `L̄_g = leaf_length · (D̄_g / leaf_diameter)^β`, and `β` has no default because
      49              :   its value depends on what one vessel stands for. A Strahler *element*, a run of segments of
      50              :   one order with its side branches, gives `β ≈ 1–1.2` (Van Bavel & Spaan 1992; Huo & Kassab
      51              :   2012), and about 2 in hamster skeletal-muscle arterioles under a dorsal skin-fold window
      52              :   (Lapi et al. 2019). A *segment* between two forks gives `β ≈ 0`: segment length barely
      53              :   tracks diameter (Wischgoll et al. 2009). A symmetric tree has no side branches, so read a
      54              :   generation as an element when the tree stands in for a deeper asymmetric one, and as a
      55              :   segment when it is the anatomy itself. With `γ = 3`, `β = 1` gives every generation the same
      56              :   pressure drop (in general `β = 4 − γ` does); `β = 0` concentrates the drop at the leaves.
      57              : - **Spread.** Each vessel's length and diameter are its generation's mean times a lognormal
      58              :   factor `exp(σ_g z − σ_g²/2)` with `σ_g² = log(1 + cv_g²)` and `z` standard normal from `rng`,
      59              :   which has mean one and coefficient of variation exactly `cv_g`. Lengths within an order are
      60              :   lognormal (Schröder et al. 1990; Kuhnle et al. 1993). Within one arteriolar network
      61              :   `cv_diameter ≈ 0.1` and `cv_length ≈ 0.2–0.4`; across networks, or by segment, lengths
      62              :   spread by 0.5–1 (Koller et al. 1987; Lapi et al. 2019; Wischgoll et al. 2009).
      63              :   `cv_diameter` and `cv_length` are each a number for every generation or a vector of
      64              :   `depth + 1`, root first. Lengths are drawn before diameters, vessel by vessel, and a quantity
      65              :   whose CV is zero everywhere draws nothing.
      66              : - **Other distributions.** To scatter by something other than a lognormal, pass
      67              :   `diameter_distribution` or `length_distribution` in place of the CV: each vessel's factor is
      68              :   then `rand(rng, d)` from its generation's `d`, any sampler such as a Distributions.jl
      69              :   distribution, or a vector of numbers to resample measured factors uniformly. Give one sampler
      70              :   for every generation or a vector of `depth + 1`, root first. The factor scales the
      71              :   generation's mean, so `d` should have mean one: a mean of `m` multiplies the whole generation
      72              :   by `m` and moves it off `γ` and `β`. Every draw must be finite and positive.
      73              : 
      74              : **Independent draws overstate heterogeneity.** Every vessel is drawn independently, so `γ`
      75              : holds generation by generation on average, not at every fork. Real trees size a vessel to the
      76              : subtree it feeds: a pig coronary tree reconstructed from per-order statistics gives a terminal
      77              : flow CV of 0.95 against 0.18–0.20 measured, and 0.22 once diameters follow the downstream tree
      78              : (Namani et al. 2018); converting measured lung diameter asymmetry through Poiseuille predicts
      79              : 136 % flow dispersion against about 35 % measured (Glenny et al. 1991). Measured CVs fed in
      80              : here therefore bound the leaf-flow heterogeneity from above rather than calibrate it.
      81              : 
      82              : **Orders are not generations.** Morphometry is tabulated by diameter-defined Strahler order,
      83              : and a real tree has about three vessels of one order per vessel of the next, not two:
      84              : branching ratios of 2.76 (Jiang et al. 1994), 3.30 (Van Bavel & Spaan 1992) and 2.72 (Lapi et
      85              : al. 2019). A per-order diameter or length ratio is therefore not a per-generation one. Carry a
      86              : table over through its exponents (`γ`, `β`) and its per-order spreads matched by diameter,
      87              : never through its order-to-order ratios.
      88              : 
      89              : ### Keyword Arguments
      90              : - `leaf_diameter`, `leaf_length`: the mean diameter and length of the last generation.
      91              : - `β`: the length–diameter exponent; required.
      92              : - `γ`: the junction exponent, positive.
      93              : - `cv_diameter`, `cv_length`: coefficient of variation per generation, `≥ 0`.
      94              : - `diameter_distribution`, `length_distribution`: the per-vessel factor's distribution, in
      95              :   place of the matching CV.
